# How would I know whether the readiness score is right?

The score is useful only if it helps learners make a better booking decision. The 80% readiness line, half credit for guesses and skill floors are cautious starting assumptions, not validated predictions.

After launch, I would recruit a pilot of 50–100 learners across all three bands. With consent, I would pair each diagnostic with their later official exam result using a random identifier, recording the provider and time between attempts. This follow-up is a proposed study; the current app collects no server-side data.

I would compare pass rates across bands and focus on the costliest error: a “Ready” learner who fails. I would report uncertainty, investigate untested skills and adjust thresholds on one group, then check them on a separate group. I would compare diagnostic reading with official reading results, account for learners who never book or do not respond, and check stability with equivalent question sets to reduce memorisation. A small pilot would guide iteration, not prove accuracy.

Before v2, I would ask five real learners:

1. After seeing your result, would you book, wait or ignore it? What makes you trust or doubt that choice?
2. Which practice action would you actually do this week? What would stop you?
3. Which question felt confusing, too easy or unlike your course or exam preparation?
4. Did you mark guesses honestly? Would you do so if a friend or teacher were watching?
5. When did you last decide whether to book an exam, and what information could have changed your decision?

I would observe each learner taking the check before asking these questions. Their answers would help prioritise question quality, clearer advice or an assessment of listening, writing and speaking. Success means fewer costly premature bookings and an achievable next step, not merely higher diagnostic scores.
